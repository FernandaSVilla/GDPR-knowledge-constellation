About & method

What this is

A working map of the obligations an organisation has to satisfy under Regulation (EU) 2016/679, arranged so that the shape of a compliance programme is visible at a glance. It is an orientation and self-assessment tool: not legal advice, and not a substitute for a qualified assessment of your own processing.

How to read the fine tiers

Each obligation is tagged 2% or 4%. This is the statutory maximum category that Article 83(4) or 83(5) assigns to that type of infringement, nothing more. It is not an estimate of any fine. Actual amounts are set case by case against the eleven criteria in Article 83(2): nature, gravity and duration, intent or negligence, mitigation, prior infringements, degree of cooperation, and the rest. Most enforcement lands far below the ceiling. The tier is here to help you prioritise, not to predict an outcome.

Regulation text vs. supervisory expectation

Not everything a supervisory authority expects is written in the Regulation. Every obligation is therefore tagged by source:

Regulation text: an express requirement in the articles cited.
CJEU case law: an obligation established by the Court, not by the text alone.
Supervisory guidance: a structured practice expected by the EDPB or its predecessor, derived from a recital or from a general duty rather than stated as a discrete requirement.

The distinction matters: guidance-derived items are strongly advisable and are what regulators look for, but they are not the same legal animal as an express article, and should not be presented as though they were.

Citations

Each obligation cites the specific provision down to paragraph and sub-paragraph where one exists, plus the relevant recital or guidance document. Verify against the consolidated text on EUR-Lex and the current EDPB guidelines before relying on anything here, guidance is revised, and the Digital Omnibus has amendments to the GDPR in negotiation.
